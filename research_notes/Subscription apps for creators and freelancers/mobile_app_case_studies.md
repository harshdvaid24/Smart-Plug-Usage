## 0. Case-study compilation (objective): which 12–20 mobile subscription apps have proof of revenue, and what are their attributes?

### Takeaway
Of the 19 apps tabulated, the clearest small-team proof-of-revenue cases are **consumer self-improvement apps grown with paid creator content**: Cal AI (>$30–40M/yr; team of 7–30; acquired by MyFitnessPal, announced Mar 2026), Umax (peak ~$700K/month, later ~$400K) and QUITTR (~$250K–500K/month). The creator-facing apps with large revenue are mostly VC-funded or owned by big companies (Captions/Mirage, ManyChat, Linktree, Picsart, InShot, Hypic). The main capital-light exception, **Stan** (~$35M ARR estimated; one $29/month tier; $5M seed), is web-first. Nearly every figure is a founder claim or a third-party estimate, and **no Indian solo or small-team mobile subscription app with verified public MRR was found**.

### Inferences
- **What makes a mobile subscription app work** (synthesised from rows 1–6 and Section 5; an inference, not a single source):
  1. **One "magic moment" that shows well in a 15–60 second video.** Examples: photo → calories (Cal AI), face → score (Umax), day → visual timeline (Structured). That moment is the ad creative.
  2. **A high-emotion personal outcome:** weight, looks, addiction, sleep, screen time. Users pay to become someone, not for a feature.
  3. **Distribution built into the product:** the app fits formats creators already make, paid on performance at low CPMs in niches with many creators and few sponsors (Cal AI: $0.50–3 CPM).
  4. **Monetize at onboarding.** Hard paywalls convert ~5x better by Day 35 and earn ~8x more revenue per install by Day 60, with similar one-year retention (RevenueCat).
  5. **Plan for decay.** Umax lost ~43% from its peak and Lensa ~98%. Winners run portfolios or exit (Cal AI → MyFitnessPal).
- **Creator-facing tools that move money for creators** (Stan's storefront at ~$437 per creator per year; ManyChat's DM-to-sale automation) look more durable than **creative-output tools**. Free platform apps commoditize editing and captions: Edits, CapCut, Hypic.
- Verifiable proof is rare. Every small-team revenue number above is founder-reported or modeled. TechCrunch said it could not validate Cal AI's claims, though the MyFitnessPal acquisition is real.
- For a Gujarat-based solo developer (React Native, Next.js, LLMs), rows 1–3 imply a **global-first, English-language, creator-marketed consumer app**, with India as a secondary, PPP-priced market (see Sections 3 and 5 for why).

### Gaps
- **Pricing and paywall type for Cal AI, QUITTR, Umax, Opal, Finch and Structured were not verified.** These apps are widely described as hard-paywall, weekly/annual subscription apps, but no source in this research confirmed it. Check their App Store in-app purchase listings before citing prices.
- No small-team creator-facing *native mobile* app with verified revenue was found. Every creator-side row is a benchmark except Stan, which is web-first.
- No Indian small-team mobile subscription app with verified revenue was found (Section 3).
- Main acquisition channels for Captions, InShot, Picsart, Linktree, Beacons, ManyChat, Opal and Finch were not captured.

## 1. Creator-facing mobile apps: what do they charge, and what do they earn / how many downloads?

### Takeaway
Creator-facing apps cluster at **~$5–30 a month**: Linktree $5 (now $8) to $24, Beacons $10, Captions up to $24.99 (Studio $89), Stan $29, Prequel ~$5.99 a week. The ones with large, citable revenue are VC-funded or giant-owned: Captions ~$28.4M trailing in-app revenue, Stan ~$35M ARR, Linktree ~$37M (2023), Picsart ~$155–265M estimated. Mobile editing is a downloads war that free or giant-owned apps win (CapCut ~509M downloads in 2025, InShot ~93M, Hypic ~37M in 2024, Edits 7.1M in its first week). **No 2023–2026 revenue or download data surfaced for Preview, Planoly, Mojo, Unfold, VN or Splice.**

### Inferences
- **Revenue per download varies hugely with user intent** (arithmetic on the cited figures). Captions earned about **$8.9 per download** ($28.4M / 3.2M, trailing year). Hypic earned about **$0.15** ($6.7M / ~44M projected 2025 downloads). Pro creators with a business reason to pay are worth far more than casual editors.
- **Money-adjacent creator tools hold value better than creative tools.** Stan (sales storefront, profitable at ~40% EBITDA on one $29 tier) and ManyChat (DM automation, near break-even at 1.5M customers) contrast with Captions, which had to go freemium against Edits and CapCut. A creator tool that touches revenue, leads or brand deals is likelier to keep paying users than one that edits video.
- **Link-in-bio is mature and crowded** (Linktree, Beacons, Stan, Whop). Linktree's Nov 2025 Starter price rise ($5 → $8) and Stan's slowdown to +24% growth point to a saturated market, not a gap.
- **Platform owners are the main competitive threat on mobile.** Meta (Edits) and ByteDance (CapCut, Hypic) give away editing and can stay unmonetized for years (Hypic). A solo developer should not build a general editor, caption app or template app.
- Story-template and planner apps (Unfold, Mojo, Preview, Planoly) were the 2017–2021 creator-app wave. Unfold exited to Squarespace in 2019. Their silence in 2023–2026 data suggests the category stalled once Instagram added native templates and scheduling. That cause is an inference, not a sourced fact.
