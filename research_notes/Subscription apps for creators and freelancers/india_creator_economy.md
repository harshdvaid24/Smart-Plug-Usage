## 1. How many creators, how many earn, earnings by tier, and influencer-marketing market size

### Takeaway
India has about 4 million creators with measurable followings: 4.12M in 2025 per ISB–Hashfame (Qoruz data), and 3.5–4.5M per Kofluence. BCG counts 2–2.5M of them as monetising, but the income is very concentrated:
- only about 8–10% monetise effectively;
- most nano and micro creators earn under ₹18,000 (~$205) a month;
- one headline on the 2026 ISB–Hashfame report says 85% of creators got no paid campaign.

Influencer-marketing spend is only about ₹3,000–4,500 crore (~$350–510M) for 2024–25, growing about 18–25% a year.

### Inferences
- The creator funnel looks like this:
  - About 4.1M creators in total.
  - About 0.6M ran at least one paid campaign in 2025. That is 15% of 4.12M if the 85% headline holds, and fits the 4.08 lakh non-metro creators in campaigns plus metro creators.
  - About 0.16–0.25M monetise effectively: 8–10% of BCG's 2–2.5M, which matches Kalaari's 150–200K professionals.
  - So the "pro" segment that can afford ₹500–1,000 a month for software is probably around 0.2M people. The mass segment (millions of creators) has very small budgets.
- Spend per paid creator is small:
  - About ₹3,500–4,500 crore of spend divided by about 0.6M paid creators is roughly ₹58,000–75,000 (~$660–850) per paid creator per year on average.
  - That average is before agency margins and is skewed toward celebrities and macro creators, so the median nano creator's brand income is far lower.
- Market-size estimates for 2025 differ by 30–50% (₹3,000–4,500 crore). They mix agency fees, creator payouts and different years. Growth of about 20–25% a year is the consensus.
- Do not mix BCG's $20–25B "direct creator-ecosystem revenue" with the influencer-marketing spend of about $0.4–0.5B. They differ by about 50x, so BCG's figure must include much broader revenue.
- Gujarat has more creators than its population share would suggest (ISB–Hashfame). A Gujarat-based developer has local access to user interviews and to Gujarati-language creators.
- The report headlines describe a two-speed market: fast growth in creator count and spend, alongside a mass of under-monetised part-timers.

## 2. Platforms Indian creators use and how they monetise

### Takeaway
- Instagram is India's largest creator platform by creator count (about 2M creators) but pays creators little directly.
- YouTube is where platform payouts happen: over ₹21,000 crore paid over three years to Indian creators, artists and media companies.
- Brand deals provide about 90% of creator revenue.
- Instagram's own subscriptions and gifts are reported as underused. Instagram creators therefore earn mainly from brand deals and from products sold off the platform (calls, courses, communities) through link-in-bio tools.
- Indian short-video apps (Moj, ShareChat) have declined. Snapchat is pushing creator programmes, but access to its payouts is limited.

### Inferences
- Instagram and Reels creators get very little income directly from the platform. Their earnings come from:
  - brand deals, both barter and paid;
  - link-in-bio sales of calls, courses, communities and digital products;
  - affiliate links (not researched).

  So their tools need to help them win and administer deals and turn followers into off-platform revenue, not chase platform payouts.
- Creators who want platform payouts move to YouTube. Vloggers probably run YouTube (long-form and Shorts) alongside Instagram Reels. That makes repurposing and cross-posting relevant, though I did not research it.
- With Moj and ShareChat declining, regional creators have probably consolidated on Instagram Reels and YouTube Shorts.

## 3. Indian creator tools and platforms: what they do, how they charge, and public figures

### Takeaway
The India-built creator monetisation stack is crowded and partly funded. It covers link-in-bio storefronts, 1:1 bookings, courses and paid communities:
- TagMango: ₹20.9 Cr FY25 revenue and profitable;
- Exly: about $8M raised, 10,000+ customers;
- Graphy: an Unacademy subsidiary that has been restructured;
- Topmate: lightly funded;
- SuperProfile, from Cosmofeed.

Influencer-marketing platforms (Kofluence, Wobb, Winkl / Good Creator Co.) are paid by brands, and their creator apps are thin; Kofluence had ₹52.5 Cr FY25 revenue and a loss. In my results I found no India-built creator-side back-office tool (deals, invoices, GST/TDS, payment follow-up).

### Inferences
- If the unverified obligations below hold, an Instagram creator doing barter and paid deals faces recurring paperwork:
  - adding disclosure labels to each post;
  - reconciling TDS deducted by brands against Form 26AS and AIS, and claiming refunds in the tax return;
  - possibly registering for GST once receipts pass ₹20 lakh;
  - raising invoices and chasing payments.

  This is document work that a mobile app with LLM extraction could handle: reading brand briefs, invoices and TDS certificates. This is a hypothesis that needs validation.

### Gaps
- **UNVERIFIED leads (verify before use):**
  - **ASCI:** the influencer advertising guidelines took effect on 14 June 2021. Disclosure labels such as #ad, #collab and #sponsored must be upfront and prominent. The guidelines were later updated for finfluencers and virtual influencers.
  - **CCPA and Department of Consumer Affairs:**
    - CCPA issued guidelines on misleading advertisements and endorsements in June 2022.
    - The Department of Consumer Affairs published its "Endorsement Know-hows!" guide for celebrities and influencers in January 2023. Disclosures must be prominent and in the language of the endorsement.
    - Penalties under the Consumer Protection Act 2019 are up to ₹10 lakh, or ₹50 lakh for repeat offences, and an endorsement ban of up to one year, or three years for repeat offences.
  - **SEBI:** in 2024, SEBI barred regulated entities from working with unregistered finfluencers.
  - **Section 194R:** from 1 July 2022, 10% TDS applies to benefits or perquisites worth more than ₹20,000 a year. CBDT Circular 12/2022 treats products given to influencers as benefits unless they are returned.
  - **TDS on fees:** brands typically deduct under Section 194J (10%) or 194C (1% or 2%). Budget 2025 raised the 194J threshold to ₹50,000.
  - **New Income-tax Act:** the Income-tax Act, 2025 replaced the 1961 Act from 1 April 2026 and renumbers sections. Check how 194R and 194J map to the new numbering.
  - **GST:**
    - Registration is required above ₹20 lakh of aggregate turnover for services, or ₹10 lakh in special-category states.
    - Promotional services are taxed at 18%.
    - YouTube AdSense receipts may count as zero-rated exports under a Letter of Undertaking.
  - **Tax return code:** income-tax return forms for assessment year 2025-26 reportedly added a profession code for social-media influencers.
  - **Late payments:** the MSMED Act's 45-day payment rule and Section 43B(h) (from FY2023-24) protect Udyam-registered micro enterprises. Whether they apply to individual creators is unclear.
  - **Tools in use:** generic invoicing apps (Zoho Invoice, Refrens, Vyapar, Khatabook), chartered accountants and spreadsheets. My results showed no creator-specific Indian compliance tool.

### Inferences
Ranking by pain × willingness to pay × feasibility for a solo React Native / Next.js / LLM developer × fit with a mobile-first product:
1. **Brand-deal back office** (#5 plus #6): a mobile app with a web dashboard; deals, invoices, GST and TDS tracking, and payment reminders through WhatsApp and email. It directly addresses money owed and draws on the Gujarat developer's local tax knowledge. Its main risk: the evidence for this pain is not sourced yet.
2. **Proof-of-value kit** (#3 plus #2): an auto-updating media kit, a rate calculator and post-campaign reports through the Instagram Graph API. Works as a viral free tier that leads into #1.
3. **Pitching copilot** (#1): LLM-drafted pitches and follow-ups with a brand-contact CRM. Risk: deliverability, and the limited ability of nano creators to pay.
4. **Regional-language captions** (#7): only if a quick test shows clearly better Hinglish or Gujarati accuracy than Meta's and YouTube's built-in tools.

Gujarat being over-represented among creators makes in-person validation interviews (for example in Ahmedabad and Surat) a cheap first step. This is an inference from ISB–Hashfame.
